## Setting Up as Self-Employed

### Registering with HMRC

You must register as self-employed with HMRC by 5 October in the tax year after you start working for yourself. If you miss this deadline you may face a penalty. Registration is free and done online through the Government Gateway at gov.uk/log-in-file-self-assessment-tax-return.

You will need your National Insurance number and a Government Gateway account. Once registered, HMRC sends a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by post within 10 working days.

### Choosing a Business Structure

Most freelancers start as sole traders because there is no registration fee and the paperwork is minimal. You report income through Self Assessment each year. The downside is unlimited personal liability: if your business has debts, your personal assets can be used to pay them.

A limited company offers liability protection and can be more tax-efficient once profits exceed roughly 30,000 GBP per year. However, you must file accounts with Companies House, run payroll for your own salary, and pay Corporation Tax. The administrative burden is higher.

## Tax Obligations

### Income Tax Bands (2024/25)

Income tax is calculated on profits after allowable expenses and the Personal Allowance.

- Personal Allowance: 12,570 GBP (no tax on this amount)
- Basic rate (20%): 12,571 to 50,270 GBP
- Higher rate (40%): 50,271 to 125,140 GBP
- Additional rate (45%): above 125,140 GBP

Your Personal Allowance reduces by 1 GBP for every 2 GBP your income exceeds 100,000 GBP.

### Payments on Account

HMRC requires two advance payments toward next year's tax bill if your Self Assessment liability exceeds 1,000 GBP. These are due on 31 January and 31 July. Each payment is half the previous year's tax bill. New freelancers are often surprised by the large combined bill in their first January, which covers the prior year's tax plus the first advance payment.

### Allowable Expenses

You can deduct costs that are wholly and exclusively for business purposes. Common deductible expenses include:

- Professional subscriptions and memberships
- Software and online tools used for work
- A portion of home broadband if you work from home
- Business travel (not commuting to a regular office)
- Professional development courses directly related to your trade
- Accountancy and bookkeeping fees

You cannot deduct personal expenses, client entertainment, or clothing (unless it is a uniform or protective gear).

## National Insurance for the Self-Employed

### Class 2 National Insurance

From April 2024, Class 2 National Insurance is no longer a separate payment. It is now included automatically within your Self Assessment calculation when your profits exceed the Small Profits Threshold (6,725 GBP for 2024/25). No action is required beyond filing your return.

### Class 4 National Insurance

Class 4 is charged on annual profits above the Lower Profits Limit.

- Lower Profits Limit: 12,570 GBP
- Rate on profits from 12,570 to 50,270 GBP: 6%
- Rate on profits above 50,270 GBP: 2%

Both Class 4 and income tax are calculated and paid together through Self Assessment.

## Banking and Payments

### Business Bank Accounts

Sole traders are not legally required to have a separate business bank account, but separating personal and business finances makes bookkeeping much easier and reduces the chance of errors in your Self Assessment.

Several UK banks and fintech providers offer free or low-cost business accounts for sole traders and small limited companies. Starling, Monzo Business, Tide, and Mettle (by NatWest) are popular options. Traditional high-street banks charge monthly fees but may offer additional services.

### Invoicing

Your invoice must include:

- Your full name (and business name if different)
- Your address
- The client's name and address
- A unique invoice number
- The date of the invoice and the payment due date
- A description of the work or goods
- The amount payable and the VAT amount (if VAT registered)

Keep copies of all invoices for at least 6 years.

## VAT

### Registration Threshold

You must register for VAT if your VAT-taxable turnover exceeds 90,000 GBP in any rolling 12-month period (2024/25 threshold). You can register voluntarily below this threshold, which allows you to reclaim VAT on business purchases.

### Making Tax Digital for VAT

All VAT-registered businesses must use HMRC-compatible accounting software and submit VAT returns digitally. Paper returns are no longer accepted. Returns are due one month and seven days after the end of each VAT period.

## Working from Home

### Flat Rate Allowance

If you work primarily from home, HMRC allows a simplified flat rate deduction instead of calculating actual costs:

- 25 to 50 hours per month: 10 GBP per month
- 51 to 100 hours per month: 18 GBP per month
- Over 100 hours per month: 26 GBP per month

### Actual Cost Method

Alternatively, you can claim a proportion of your actual home costs (rent or mortgage interest, council tax, utility bills) based on the fraction of your home used for work and the number of hours worked. This requires more detailed records but may produce a higher deduction.

## Pensions

Self-employed workers are not automatically enrolled in a pension. Contributing to a personal pension or self-invested personal pension (SIPP) provides immediate tax relief at your marginal rate. Contributions are capped at the Annual Allowance (60,000 GBP for 2024/25 or 100% of earnings, whichever is lower).

A private pension is the primary vehicle for retirement savings when you are self-employed. Starting early and contributing consistently has a significant impact on the final pot due to compound growth over time.
